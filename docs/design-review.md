# L-Shaped EKBACKEN Desk — Design Review

Oct 19, 2026 · @Scott Howson

## Verdict in five lines

1. Buy neither the C-channels nor the four square tubes. Buy two 7 ft lengths of 2 × 2 × 3/16 in A36 steel angle (about 14 ft total, roughly CAD $50–75 at a steel yard — price to confirm with Acier Terrebonne).
2. One angle rail runs under each top's front edge, set 12 cm back, and the two rails are bolted together at the inside corner with a short offcut of the same angle. That bolted joint, not the joining plates, carries the inside corner.
3. Seven OLOV legs (your six plus one more, $15) go along the back wall edge and the side wall edge, none in the knee zone; the two ALEX units, each on four 4 in round adjustable feet, sit centred under the two far ends with 3.5 cm of top showing all round. Set every OLOV to 80.2 cm so the tops sit level on the spacers: top surface at 83.0 cm. You sit at the inside corner facing the diagonal: the Samsung M7 hangs across the corner, the two Acers wrap along each wall with an Edifier under each, and the Y60 sits over ALEX 1. The tops stand 5 cm off both walls so the VIVO clamps have room behind the edges.
4. Every screw into the top is a #10 × 1 in wood screw in a pilot hole, penetrating ≤ 21 mm of the 28 mm top; no fastener reaches the laminate.
5. Expected worst-case flex at the inside corner is about 2–3 mm (my estimate, not verified); add an eighth OLOV under either rail only if that bothers you.

Everything below separates **IKEA's published guidance** from **my engineering estimates**, and ends with the measurements you still need to take before drilling.

## A. Recommended design: back-edge legs plus two bolted front rails

The design puts every leg against a wall edge, carries both front edges on steel angle rails, and ties the rails together at the inside corner with a bolted bracket. The joining plates stay, but only to keep the two surfaces flush.

**Why legs can't be where IKEA's rule wants them.** IKEA's 32 in spacing assumes supports that run the full depth of the top (cabinets, or leg pairs front and back). A leg only at the back leaves the front edge hanging 55 cm beyond it, far past the 10 in overhang limit. Legs at the front edge every 32 in would sit exactly where your knees go. So the front edge needs a beam, not more legs: a rail that spans from the inside corner to each ALEX and is supported by them.

**What carries what (load path).**

- Back edge of top A and wall edge of top B: four OLOV legs on A (two of them in the corner square, directly under the monitor mount) and three on B at ≤ 75 cm spacing, plus the ALEX units at each end. This meets IKEA's spacing rule along those edges.
- Front edge of top A: rail A, a 2 × 2 × 3/16 in angle, the full 213 cm, screwed to the underside 12 cm back from the edge; the 10 cm feet lift the top enough that the rail passes over the ALEX with 5 cm to spare. Its ends rest, through the top, on leg L2 at the wall and on ALEX 1. The 12 cm strip beyond the rail is a cantilever within IKEA's 25 cm limit.
- Front edge of top B: rail B, same angle, 213 cm, supported at ALEX 2 and, at the corner, by a bracket bolted to rail A. Rail B also crosses the joint, so it acts as a third, much stiffer joining plate.
- Inside corner of top B: rail B → bolted bracket → rail A → L1 and ALEX 1. Steel-to-steel bolts, no particleboard screw in the primary path.
- Racking: the two ALEX boxes, screwed to the tops at opposite ends of the L, brace the whole frame; the telescoping OLOV legs contribute even less against sideways push than ADILS would.

**Why angle instead of square tube.** For the same wall thickness, a 2 in angle is stiffer than a 1¼ in square tube (moment of inertia 0.27 in⁴ vs about 0.12 in⁴), it costs about the same per foot, and it needs one hole per screw instead of a clearance hole in one wall and a larger access hole in the opposite wall. The vertical leg also gives you a flat face to bolt the corner bracket to. The trade-off is 5.1 cm of depth below the top, versus 3.2 cm for the tube; section E covers the knee-clearance check this forces.

**Why not a wood apron.** A 2 × 4 on edge is as stiff and cheaper, but it hangs 9 cm, needs pocket screws or brackets to fasten without a jig, and looks like what it is. If the clearance check in E fails for a 2 in angle, a 1½ × 1½ × 3/16 in angle (3.8 cm deep) is the fallback, with about 70% more flex.

**Rejected alternatives.**

- C-channel stiffeners (Option A): made to be let into a ¼ in × ¾ in dado in a slab 1¼ in thick or more ([Lee Valley](https://www.leevalley.com/en-ca/shop/hardware/table-hardware/brackets-and-supports/116623-c-channel-tabletop-stiffeners)). A 28 mm particleboard top can't take that dado. Surface-mounted they are just an expensive 2 × 1 in channel, four 52 in pieces don't span your 208 and 213 cm runs without splicing, and the Canadian-made 52 in version lists at CAD $79.95 each ([KJP Select Hardwoods](https://kjpselecthardwoods.com/products/c-channel)). About $320 for a worse fit.
- Four 6 ft square tubes (Option B): workable, but 24 ft is nearly double what the layout needs, the 1¼ in section is less stiff than the angle, and every fastener needs two holes. If you strongly prefer the look, buy two 7 ft tubes of 1½ × 1½ × ⅛ in (0.22 in⁴) and follow the same layout; the drilling steps in D note the differences.
- A leg at the inside corner: solves everything structurally and is exactly what you asked to avoid. Kept only as the optional eighth-leg upgrade in E.
- A commercial frame: not needed; the parts you own can be made to work.

**Steps.**

1. Cut only the 9 cm bracket from the 1 ft offcut; both rails are used at the full 213 cm. File the cut and both ends of each rail.
2. Mark the hole schedule on the flat legs with a square and tape measure, centre-punch each mark, and drill the 7/32 in holes at low speed with oil. Drill the two 9/32 in bolt holes in each rail's vertical leg the same way. Deburr every hole with the file or a larger bit turned by hand.
3. Degrease with acetone, scuff the mill scale, spray two light coats of enamel, and let them cure 24 h. Mask nothing; the holes clear themselves.
4. Top A face down on a blanket. Mark the four leg centres, the four feet centres under the ALEX 1 footprint, the two clamp zones (x 31–39 on the back edge and y 31–39 on the left end, keep 9 cm in from the edge clear) and the rail line (vertical leg at 12 cm from the front edge). Fit OLOV L1, L2, L3 and L4 with the IKEA screws after a 1/8 in pilot in each hole, and pre-set each leg to 80.2 cm. Screw the four feet plates to the underside at their centres, pads pointing away from the top, and run each pad out so the foot measures 10.2 cm overall. Clamp rail A on its line, wall end flush with the top's end, and drill the nine 1/8 in pilots through the rail holes with a tape depth stop 25 mm from the bit's tip (4.8 mm steel plus 20 mm). Drive the #10 × 1 in screws with the bit extension; stop when the head seats. A stripped hole in particleboard is not recoverable, so set the drill clutch low.
5. Top B face down. Fit L5, L6, L7 and the four feet for ALEX 2 the same way. Clamp rail B on its line with the corner end 11 cm past the top's end (that 11 cm will sit under top A). Pilot and screw holes 3 through 10 only.
6. Set both ALEX units in the room, ALEX 1 at x 209.5–245.5, y 3.5–61.5 (drawers toward you). With a helper, stand top A on L1–L4 with the four feet pads landing on ALEX 1's top, and adjust the OLOV legs and the feet until a level reads flat and all four pads bear. Fix ALEX 1 with two corner braces at its back edge (#8 × 5/8 in into the top, #8 × ½ in into the ALEX panel).
7. Stand top B on L5–L7 and ALEX 2 (x 3.5–61.5, y 274.5–310.5, door toward you), slide it until its end meets A's front edge with the two surfaces flush, and adjust feet. Screw two of the 10 × 3 in mending plates across the joint from below, long axis across the joint, centred at x = 15 and 35 cm, with #10 × ⅝ in screws and washers, pilots 12 mm deep.
8. Push rail B's corner end against rail A's vertical leg. Drill the two remaining 1/8 in pilots through rail B's holes 1 and 2 into top A, 20 mm deep, and screw.
9. Clamp the bracket into the outside corner where the two vertical legs meet, with its heel at the corner and a leg flat on each rail. Drill 9/32 in through the bracket using the rail holes as the guide. Fit the four ¼-20 bolts with a washer under each head and nut and nylon-insert nuts, snug.
10. Fix ALEX 2 with two braces. Retighten every screw after two weeks, as IKEA advises for ALEX and OLOV. Then clamp one VIVO pole at x 35 on A's back edge and the other at y 35 on A's left end edge, a spare 10 × 3 in plate flat under each jaw, so the crossbar runs across the corner. ## E. Structural assessment

The six-leg plan without rails is inadequate: both front edges would hang 55 cm past the nearest leg, more than twice IKEA's 25 cm limit, and the inside corner of top B would have no vertical support at all. The rails and the bolted corner bracket are the minimum change that fixes both. Everything below is my estimate from standard beam formulas with A36 steel (E = 200 GPa) and a 28 mm particleboard top; nothing here has been load-tested.

| Failure mode | Finding | Margin or number | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sag at the inside corner | Rail A spans 196 cm between L2 and ALEX 1 and carries rail B's corner reaction 44 cm from L2 | about 2–3 mm worst case with 2 × 2 × 3/16 angle (≈ 3.5 mm with ⅛ wall, ≈ 5.5 mm with 1¼ in tube) | acceptable; the optional eighth leg below cuts it by half |
| Sag along the front of B | Rail B spans 220 cm between the bracket and ALEX 2 | about 2.5 mm with 15 kg leaning mid-span | acceptable |
| Rail overload | 80 kg person sitting mid-span on rail A | bending stress ≈ 130 MPa vs 250 MPa yield, margin ≈ 1.9 | fine, but don't sit on the inside corner |
| Top between supports | 38 cm from back legs to rail, 28 mm board, laminated both faces | far below the 80 cm IKEA span | none |
| Overhang | 12 cm beyond rails, 3.5 cm beyond ALEX, 10–14 cm beyond wall legs | all within 25 cm | none |
| Fastener pull-out | #10 screws, 20 mm in particleboard, roughly 50–80 kg each (typical published range); 9–10 per rail | rails see mostly shear, not pull-out | none; the only pull-out case is the two screws into A's front strip, which the bolted bracket backs up |
| Joint movement | Joint bridged by rail B (steel, 11 cm into A), two 10 × 3 in plates, and legs L2 and L5 within 25 cm either side | particleboard moves little with humidity | shim the two tops flush before fitting the plates |
| Racking (sideways push) | OLOV legs give almost no racking resistance; the two ALEX boxes, 2.6 m apart, fixed to the tops, do | adequate once both ALEX units are braced to the tops | don't skip the four corner braces |
| Tipping | Mass ≈ 110 kg with ALEX units, low and spread; no support more than 30 cm inboard of any edge | not a risk | none |
| ALEX capacity | IKEA lists 12 kg max load on the drawer unit | each ALEX carries roughly a quarter of the top weight (≈ 15 kg) plus what sits above it | confirm the figure on IKEA Canada's page; IKEA sells this unit as a desk support, so I read 12 kg as the load on its own top, not a desk limit |
| Knee and thigh clearance | With the spacers the rail bottom sits at 75.1 cm, 12 cm back from the front edge | clears any seated thigh | resolved by the spacers; the 1½ in fallback is no longer needed |

**OLOV rating.** 13 kg each, 91 kg for seven, against roughly 40–50 kg actually resting on the legs once the monitor array is up: a margin near 2. OLOV's inner tube can wobble a little at full extension; at 80.2 cm it sits at about two-thirds of travel, and the ALEX boxes and rails do the racking work as before.

**Triple-monitor mount (new).** Three 32 in panels plus the VIVO poles put about 30 kg on one clamp at A's back edge, with the screens 15–25 cm in front of the poles: a steady tipping moment of roughly 50–70 N·m on the clamp. Three consequences: L1 goes at (14, 14), in the corner square right under the mount, and L2 at (14, 43), so each clamp is within 25 cm of a leg (estimated deflection under a clamp under 1 mm); each clamp jaw gets one of your spare 10 × 3 in plates as a pad, because particleboard crushes under a bare steel jaw over months; and both clamps sit on top A's own edges, never on the joint. The corner arrangement puts the VIVO crossbar on the diagonal between the poles. VIVO's manual (STAND-V300CU REV1.1) shows this corner mounting, clamps on the two edges directly across the corner, and the clamps are independent C-clamps with no base bar. The pole spacing is fixed by the crossbar (23 in overall per the spec, so about 54–58 cm collar to collar, against the 42 cm assumed here); once measured, move both clamps the same amount along their edges (clamp centre = pole inset + pole spacing ÷ 1.414 from the corner). Keep the centre screen's weight within the mount's rating (59.4 lb centre; the M7 32 in is about 6–7 kg) and both Acers under 17.6 lb each (about 6 kg each with stands removed).

**Optional eighth and ninth legs. An OLOV at (165, 48) under rail A**, just outside the knee zone and 48 cm from ALEX 1, cuts rail A's corner sag roughly in half; one at (48, 165) under rail B cuts rail B's sag by about 85%. Each is CAD $15 and four screws. Build without them first; add one only if the flex bothers you.

**What this assessment does not cover.** Actual ALEX load rating, actual OLOV plate size and screw length, the spacers' footprint and fastening, actual particleboard density and screw-holding of your specific tops, and the floor being level. Those are the measurements in section G.
