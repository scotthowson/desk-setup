E · Steel rail fabrication and drilling

All steel work happens on the bench, before either rail goes near a top. Under the desk you only drive screws and bolts; you never drill steel there. Two identical lengths of 2 × 2 × 3/16 in hot-rolled angle arrive; either can be rail A. Tape readings in cm, caliper and drill sizes in mm.

!
MEASURE BEFORE MARKING OR DRILLING MEASURE ON HARDWARE
1 · Tape each rail from end to end and write the length on the tape label. Design length 213 cm; bought as 84 in (2133.6 mm, so about 213.4 cm). If a rail is under 213 cm, stop and check before drilling.
2 · Calipers on each angle: legs 50.8 mm, thickness 4.76 mm (the docs expect about 4.8 mm).
3 · Every station is measured from the labelled end: rail A's wall end, rail B's corner end. The far end carries any extra length. Never centre the hole pattern on the bar and never shift a hole to share out the extra.
1
Label the datum ends. Wrap tape round one end of each rail and write on it: RAIL A · WALL END on one, RAIL B · CORNER END on the other. Measure both rails from those ends (Fig. E-1). File both cut ends of each rail smooth.

Pilot the screw holes through the drill jig. Jig lip down over the flat leg's free edge, sight grooves on the station line, hold it down and drill 1/8 in through its bore: square, on the 19 mm line, no punch needed. All 19 stations (9 on rail A, 10 on rail B). Use a drill press if you have one.

THE FITTING IS THE TEMPLATE VERIFIED
The fittings were measured on 23 Oct 2026: legs 100.5 × 41, two 21 × 12 slots per leg with centres 29.15 and 78.45 mm from the heel. That puts the six bolt holes at the positions below. Even so, do not lay them out with the tape: clamp the fitting on its heel mark and drill through it with the printed slot plug (steps 6 and 7), so each hole lands on its slot's centre. Check first that a plug drops into each slot without rattle. The heel of a fitting is the outside of its bend.

!
WHY THE HOLES ON BOTH RAILS MEET WITHOUT A JIG VERIFIED
1 · The same fitting is the template for all three placements: rail A heel on the 52.0 cm mark legs toward the far end, rail A heel on the 51.5 cm mark legs toward the wall end, rail B heel flush with the corner end.
2 · The slot plug puts every pilot on a slot centre, 29.15 and 78.45 mm from wherever the heel sat. So when a fitting is bolted on, its slots sit over the holes it made.
3 · Both fittings are identical, so the inside one fits the holes the outside one made.
4 · What makes the two rails meet at the corner is rail B's end against rail A's vertical leg with B's outer face on the 52.0 cm mark. The floor test (step 8) proves it before painting: every bolt must slide in by hand. If one won't, open that rail hole with a round file now, never under the desk.
The rail drill jig is only for the 19-line screw holes, on either rail; it plays no part in the bolt holes.

8
Floor test. Lay both rails upside down on the floor, flat legs down, vertical legs up. Butt rail B's corner end against the outer face of rail A's vertical leg so that rail B's outer face is on rail A's 52.0 cm mark; rail B's flat leg then covers 46.9 to 52.0 cm on rail A's tape. Bolt both fittings on loosely: the outside one on the far-end side of rail B, the inside one on the wall side. Every bolt must slide in by hand. Check the corner with the combination square. If a bolt will not go in, open that hole with a round file now, not under the desk.

9
Finish. Take the fittings off. Degrease both rails, scuff with 120 grit, two coats of flat black enamel, 24 h to cure. The holes need no masking.
The rails are now ready for the tops: rail A goes on top A with its wall end at x = 1 cm (1 cm in from the taped end), rail B on top B with its corner end projecting 12 cm past the joint end. Chapter F bolts the corner once both tops stand.

F · Steel corner assembly

The two rails are joined at the inside corner by two gusseted strut fittings, one each side of rail B's end, bolted to the vertical legs. This bolted joint, not the joining plates, carries the inside corner of top B. Both tops are standing (build manual step 7); you work from below.

!
SEQUENCE: THE FITTINGS GO ON LAST SOURCE-DEFINED
Before the first fitting goes on, all of these must be done:
1 · The two surfaces are flush at the joint, leveled with the feet (never packed).
2 · Rail B's end touches the outer face of rail A's vertical leg with no gap, and rail B's outer face is on the 52.0 cm mark on rail A.
3 · Joining plates P1 and P2 are screwed across the joint. They align the tops; they do not carry the corner.
4 · Rail B's screws 1 and 2 are driven into top A. The inside fitting sits right under them and would block the driver.

Check again, then tighten all six. Fingertip across the joint: no step. Rail B's end still touching rail A. Then tighten every bolt fully:
!
TIGHTENING: 25 N·m (18 ft·lb) SOURCE-DEFINED
With a torque wrench, 25 N·m (18 ft·lb), under the standard dry torque even for a grade 2 bolt. Without one, pull as hard as one hand can on a 15 cm wrench, then stop.
Why tight, not snug: this is a friction joint. The fittings' 21 × 12 slots leave 2.5 mm of play across and 11.5 mm along each bolt, the 7/16 in rail holes 1.6 mm, so only the clamping force keeps rail B's corner from settling.
When: only after the two surfaces are flush and rail B's end touches rail A.
Later: if the corner ever drops a millimetre, retighten these six bolts.

How the rails hold the tops

Each rail is screwed to its top through the flat leg: nine #10 × 1 in pan-head Robertson screws (H03) in rail A, ten in rail B, 19 in all. Rail B's holes 1 and 2 land in top A (its first 12 cm lies under top A) and are driven in build-manual step 7.4, before the fittings; the rest go in with the tops face down.
