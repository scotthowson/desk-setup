B · Before you begin

Read this chapter once before you pick up a drill. Budget a weekend: the steel on day one, the assembly on day two.

Safety

Fig. B-1 · The five rules that protect the tops and you.
!
NEVER A FASTENER DEEPER THAN 21 MM INTO A TOP
The EKBACKEN tops are 28 mm particleboard with a thin laminate skin. A screw or pilot that reaches the skin lifts it into a bump you will see and feel for ever. Every screw in this manual stops at least 7 mm short of the top face (28 − 21). If your tops measure 27 mm instead of 28, shorten every screw into the top by 1 mm. If you ever substitute a screw, keep this rule. Chapter D shows every depth.
  • Laminate protection. Spread a blanket on the floor first. The tops lie face down on it, laminate on the blanket, for every marking and drilling step. Never slide or drag steel (rails, fittings, the clamp plates) across the laminate: lift it on and off.
  • Eye protection for every hole drilled in steel; drill at low speed with cutting oil. Gloves while you deburr and file the steel: drilled edges are sharp.
  • Two people to turn a top over and stand it on its legs (249 cm long). A helper is also needed to slide the finished desk off and back to the walls.

Work area

Fig. B-2 · Floor needed: 252 × 317 cm, including the 3 cm gaps to both walls.

The finished desk needs 252 × 317 cm of clear floor in the corner: each top is 249 cm long and stands 3 cm off its wall. MEASURE ON HARDWARE Measure both walls (wall to wall) and the baseboard thickness before you start.

You also need floor space for one top at a time lying face down on the blanket (249 × 65 cm plus room to kneel all round), and a bench with two clamps for drilling the two 213 cm steel rails. All steel is drilled on the bench, never under the desk.

Clear the corner before chapter G: the ALEX units are placed by the walls before the tops go on.

Printed tools: how each one is used

1 · Edge T-square

Fig. B-4 · The T-square on a face-down top, lip over the edge, at its station. Hole names are mm from the edge.
  1. Turn it over so the lip hangs down. Hook the lip over the edge named in the marking table.
  2. Slide the head along the edge until the blade's centre line sits on the station you ticked with the tape.
  3. Put the scriber through the hole the table names: 50 ring centres on the ALEX (5 cm in), 85 feet (8.5 cm in), 100 legs L2–L6 (10 cm in), 150 leg L1 (15 cm in). Each hole is named for its distance from the edge in mm.
  4. The four holes round 85 are 1/8 in drill holes for a feet plate (46.8 mm apart): start the four pilots through them, lift, finish with the 15 LEG puck.
  5. First-print check: hook it over a top's edge: the 100 hole must sit 10.0 cm from the edge. If not, re-print it, never scaled.

2 · Leg jig

Fig. B-5 · The leg jig hooked over the wall (unfinished) edge.
  1. Turn it over (lip down). Hook the lip over the wall edge and slide it to the leg's station.
  2. L2–L6: the 100 pattern: plain holes, solid circle, one hole toward the wall. L1: the 150 pattern: ringed holes, dashed circle.
  3. Start the five 1/8 in pilots 3–4 mm deep through the pattern's holes.
  4. The small centre hole is for a scriber only; never drill it.
  5. Lift the jig and finish each pilot with the 15 LEG puck.

3 · Feet corner jig

Fig. B-6 · The feet corner jig on a far corner of a face-down top, fences against both edges.
  1. Turn it over (fences down). Drop it over a far corner of the top so both fences bear on the two edges.
  2. It gives the foot centre 8.5 cm from both edges and the plate's four holes, 46.8 mm apart, square to the edges.
  3. Start the four 1/8 in pilots through it, lift it, finish with the 15 LEG puck.
  4. It does F2, F4, F7, F8. The other four feet (F1, F3, F5, F6) are 34.5 cm from the end: the T-square's 85 pad does those, at the tape stations in chapter D.

4 · ALEX corner template

Fig. B-7 · The template on an ALEX top corner. Black: the three ring holes you drill. White: the ALEX-foot holes.
  1. Turn it over (fences down). Drop it over a corner of a cabinet's top, fences against both edges: ring centre 5 cm from both edges.
  2. Start the three outer pilots (5/64 in, radius 33 mm) through it. Lift it, finish with the 12 ALEX puck.
  3. The inner three (radius 14.5 mm) are only for the optional ALEX feet, marked on the bottom corners. Do not drill them on the top.
  4. Set the ring on the corner and turn it until its three countersunk holes sit on the three pilots; it only fits one way round. Same template for all eight corners.

5 · Rail marking gauge

Fig. B-8 · The angle end-on with the gauge in its two positions.
  1. Turn it over (lip down). Lay it on a face of the angle with the lip over an edge; the lip's inner face is the zero.
  2. Screw line: lip over the flat leg's free edge, pencil in the 19 mm notch, slide the full length.
  3. Bolt line: lip over the vertical leg's heel edge, pencil in the 29 mm notch, slide.
  4. Use a 0.8 mm pencil point: the notches put it on exactly 19.0 and 29.0 mm.

6 · Rail drill jig

Fig. B-9 · The rail drill jig on the flat leg (seen from above), lip under the free edge.
  1. Turn it over (lip down). Hook the lip over the flat leg's free edge.
  2. Slide it until the sight grooves on both sides sit on the station line.
  3. Hold it down and drill the 1/8 in pilot through the bore: it is on the 19 mm screw line, square, and cannot wander.
  4. Pull the jig off. Open the hole to 7/32 in without the jig; the bit follows the pilot. Nineteen holes, one jig.

7 · Slot plug (fitting-centring-plug)

Fig. B-10 · A fitting clamped on the rail's vertical leg, heel on its mark, plug in the first slot.
  1. Clamp the fitting to the rail with its heel on the mark from chapter E.
  2. Drop a plug into the first slot. Drill the 1/8 in pilot through its bore. Move the plug to the next slot and repeat.
  3. Take the fitting off. Open each pilot to 7/16 in; the bit follows the pilot.
  4. Why: the slots are 21 × 12 mm, so a bolt or a punch could sit anywhere along the slot. The plug fills it and puts every pilot on the slot centre.

8 · Pilot drill guide

Fig. B-11 · (a) Setting the bit in the SET tower. (b) Drilling through a puck.
  1. Set the bit: loosen the chuck, drop the bit tip-first into the SET tower's hole for it (45 mm for 1/8 in, 35 mm for 5/64 in), rest the chuck nose on the tower, tighten.
  2. Drill: stand the puck for that hole flat on the surface (or on the plate, or on the rail's flat leg), bit down its bore, drill until the chuck nose lands on the puck.
  3. Pucks: the number is the hole depth in mm. 12 PLATE (1/8 in, joining plates) · 15 LEG (1/8 in, OLOV and feet plates) · 10 EDGE (5/64 in, trim screws sideways into the raw edge, bit horizontal) · 12 ALEX (5/64 in, rings and ALEX feet) · 25 RAIL (1/8 in, through the angle, 20 mm into the wood).
  4. Re-set the bit every time you change it. A domed chuck nose: drill one test hole in scrap and measure it.

9 · Foot height gauge

Fig. B-12 · The C gauge on a round foot: plate face to pad bottom.
  1. Stand the foot on its pad.
  2. Slide the C over it from the side.
  3. Wind the pad until the gauge just slides on with no rock: 102.0 mm inside.
  4. All eight feet are then identical, no ruler.

10 · Wall gauge

Fig. B-13 · The two legs of the wall gauge in the gap.
  1. Hold the 65 leg (6.5 cm) between the wall and a cabinet's back while you place it, and again when you slide the desk back after the trim.
  2. Hold the 30 leg (3 cm) between the wall and a top's raw edge to check the 3 cm gap.
  3. No tape needed: it just fits, or it does not.

D · Component preparation

This chapter marks both tops face down and labels the two rails. Nothing is fixed yet. Positions are in cm, as the tape reads them; thicknesses, holes and screws in mm. Every position is measured from one datum: top A's back-left corner. x runs along the back wall, y along the side wall. Top A covers x 0–249 cm, y 0–65 cm; top B covers x 0–65 cm, y 65–314 cm.

Fig. D-1 · The datum and the two tops, seen from above as installed. SOURCE-DEFINED

Which edge is which

Each top has one rounded, laminated long edge: the front. The other long edge is square and unfinished: it goes against the wall. Either short end can become the side-wall end.

Which top is A

The two tops (P01, P02) are identical. A is the one you lay along the back wall; its unfinished edge is y = 0. B lies along the side wall; its unfinished edge is x = 0 and one short end, the joint end, butts against A's front edge. Top B is top A turned 90°, never mirrored.

Fig. D-2 · Face-down convention used on every marking drawing. Stand opposite the taped reference edge (top A: at its front edge; top B: at its far end, joint end away from you): the underside is the plan mirrored left to right, so x grows to the LEFT and the datum side (x = 0) is on your RIGHT. Top B uses the same mapping.

Never through the laminate

!
NEVER THROUGH THE LAMINATE
No screw tip and no pilot goes deeper than 21 mm into a 28 mm top: at least 7 mm of wood must remain under the laminate face. Set every pilot depth with the drill guide's puck, never by eye. If a top measures 27 mm, shorten every screw into it by 1 mm.

Setting a puck

11
Loosen the chuck. Drop the bit tip-first into the SET tower's hole for that bit (45 mm for 1/8 in, 35 mm for 5/64 in), rest the chuck nose on the tower, tighten. Re-set every time you change bits.
12
Stand the puck flat on the surface (on the rail's flat leg for 25 RAIL), bit down its bore, drill until the chuck nose lands on the puck. The hole is then the depth written on the puck.

If your chuck nose is domed, drill one test hole in scrap and measure it first.
