D · Component preparation

This chapter marks both tops face down and labels the two rails. Nothing is fixed yet. Every position is in mm from one datum: top A's back-left corner. x runs along the back wall, y along the side wall. Top A covers x 0–2490, y 0–650; top B covers x 0–650, y 650–3140.

Fig. D-1 · The datum and the two tops, seen from above as installed. SOURCE-DEFINED

Which edge is which

Each top has one rounded, laminated long edge: the front. The other long edge is square and unfinished: it goes against the wall. Either short end can become the side-wall end.

Which top is A

The two tops (P01, P02) are identical. A is the one you lay along the back wall; its unfinished edge is y = 0. B lies along the side wall; its unfinished edge is x = 0 and one short end, the joint end (y = 650), butts against A's front edge. Top B is top A turned 90°, never mirrored.

Fig. D-2 · Face-down convention used on every marking drawing. Stand opposite the taped reference edge (top A: at its front edge; top B: at its far end, joint end away from you): the underside is the plan mirrored left to right, so x grows to the LEFT and the datum side (x = 0) is on your RIGHT. Top B uses the same mapping.

Never through the laminate

!
NEVER THROUGH THE LAMINATE
No screw tip and no pilot goes deeper than 21 mm into a 28 mm top: 28 − 21 = 7 mm of wood must remain under the laminate face. Set every pilot depth with the drill guide's puck, never by eye. If a top measures 27 mm, shorten every screw into it by 1 mm.

Setting a puck

11
Loosen the chuck. Drop the bit tip-first into the SET tower's hole for that bit (45 for 1/8 in, 35 for 5/64 in), rest the chuck nose on the tower, tighten. Re-set every time you change bits.
12
Stand the puck flat on the surface (on the rail's flat leg for 25 RAIL), bit down its bore, drill until the chuck nose lands on the puck. The hole is then the depth written on the puck.

If your chuck nose is domed, drill one test hole in scrap and measure it first.
