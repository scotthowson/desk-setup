0 · How this desk works, in plain words

Read this once before you pick up a drill. Everything after it is a list of numbers; this is why the numbers are what they are.

  1. Two kitchen counter tops make an L. Top A lies along the back wall. Top B lies along the side wall and its end pushes against the front edge of top A. They are the same size; only their direction differs. The place where they meet, in the crook of the L, is where you sit; this guide calls it the L corner.
  2. What holds them up. At the two far ends of the L, each top rests on an ALEX cabinet. Along the two wall edges there are six IKEA OLOV legs. That leaves the front edges, the ones your knees go under, with nothing beneath them for over two metres. Counter tops sag and eventually break like that.
  3. So each front edge gets a steel rail. A piece of steel angle, 2 inches on each side and 213 cm long, is screwed to the underside of each top, 12 cm back from the front edge. Steel on edge is very stiff; the rails carry the front edges the way a beam carries a floor. Where the two rails meet under the L corner they are bolted to each other with two heavy steel corner fittings, so the corner of top B hangs from rail A instead of from nothing.
  4. Why the cabinets have feet on them. An ALEX is 70 cm tall. You want the desk surface at 83 cm. Each cabinet therefore carries four 10 cm adjustable feet between its top and the desk. The feet are screwed to the desk, and their round pads drop into little printed rings screwed to the cabinet. The rings stop the desk sliding on the cabinets, and because the cabinets are heavy boxes on the floor, that also stops the whole desk from rocking sideways; the thin OLOV legs could not do that on their own.
  5. Why the legs are where they are. All six legs stand on the wall edges, none under the front. That keeps the space under the L corner, where you sit, completely open for your knees. One leg sits right under the monitor mount's second clamp, because that clamp carries the weight of the screens.
  6. Why no screw goes deeper than 21 mm. The tops are 28 mm particleboard with a thin laminate skin. A screw that reaches the skin leaves a bump you will see and feel for ever. Every screw length in this guide was chosen so that it stops at least 7 mm short of the top face. Keep that rule if you ever substitute a screw.
  7. Why the tops are drawn mirrored. You mark the undersides with the tops face down. Looking at a face-down top you see its layout flipped left-to-right, like a mirror. Sections 3 and 4 draw each top exactly as you will see it on the blanket, and tell you where to stand, so you can hold the page next to the top and the drawing matches.

Words used in this guide

Angle
Steel bent into an L. One side is 2 inches, the other side is 2 inches, the steel is 3/16 inch (4.8 mm) thick. The drawing below shows it end-on.
Flat leg
The side of the angle that lies flat against the underside of the top. All the screws go through it.
Vertical leg
The side of the angle that hangs straight down toward the floor. The corner fittings bolt to it.
Outer face, inner face
Of the vertical leg: the outer face looks toward the front edge of the desk, away from the flat leg. The inner face is the other side, tucked under the flat leg.
Heel of the angle
The bend, where the two legs meet. The heel edge is the top edge of the vertical leg, flush with the underside of the desk.
Heel of a fitting
The outside of its bend, the corner you can run a finger round. When a step says "heel on the mark", put that outside corner on the mark with the fitting's leg lying flat on the steel.
Free edge
The outer edge of a leg, the one that is not the heel. The screw holes sit 19 mm in from the flat leg's free edge.
Station
A distance along an edge, measured from the labelled end of that edge with the tape. On a rail it is where a hole goes. On a top: tick the edge at the station, draw a short line square to the edge with the combination square, and lay the T-square so its row of holes sits on that line.
Scribe
Draw a fine line with a sharp pencil or a nail.
Pilot hole
A small hole drilled first so the screw goes in straight and does not split the board. Into the desk tops it is a 1/8 in bit; into the ALEX tops a 5/64 in bit.
Drill guide
The printed stepped block. It keeps the bit square and stops the chuck at the right depth; section 1 says how to set it.
Tape flag
A piece of masking tape wrapped round the drill bit at a set distance from the tip; when the tape touches the surface, stop. The drill guide replaces this; it is the fallback if you have no guide to hand.
Centre punch
A pointed steel tool (a nail works). Tap it on a mark on steel to make a dent, so the drill bit starts exactly there instead of skating. On the tops, a pencil dot through the tool's hole is enough.
Countersunk
A hole with a cone at its mouth so a flat-head screw sits flush.
Deburr
Drilling leaves a sharp ring of metal around a hole. Rub it off with a file or twist a bigger bit in the hole by hand.
Flush
Level with, no step.
Butt
Push end-on against, no gap.
Seats
A screw head seats when it touches the metal and the plate no longer moves. Stop there.
Tight (the six corner bolts)
With a torque wrench, 25 N·m (18 ft·lb); without one, one hand's full pull on a 15 cm wrench, then stop. These bolts hold the corner by friction, because the fittings' 21 × 12 mm slots leave 2.5 mm of play across and 11.5 along each bolt, so "snug" is wrong here. Every screw into wood, by contrast, stops when its head seats.
Clutch
The numbered ring behind the drill's chuck. Start at the lowest number that still drives the screw fully home. A screw that stops a millimetre proud is fine; a stripped hole is not.
Chuck nose
The flat front face of the drill's chuck, the part that spins round the bit.
Nylon-insert nut
A nut with a plastic ring inside so it cannot vibrate loose. It is stiff to turn; that is normal.
Toe-in
Turning a side monitor or speaker a little toward you instead of flat along the wall.
Gusseted fitting
A steel L-shaped bracket with a triangular web across its inside corner. Very stiff. Ours are made for 1-5/8 in strut channel: legs 100.5 long and 41 wide, 5 mm steel, with two 21 × 12 mm slots in each leg centred 29 and 78 mm from the heel (measured 23 Oct 2026).
Fence
A raised lip on a printed template that rests against an edge.
Racking
The whole desk leaning sideways like a parallelogram. The cabinets, locked to the tops by the rings, stop it.

The angle rail seen end-on, as it sits under the top, and the two lines everything is measured from: the screw line 19 mm in from the flat leg's free edge, and the bolt line 29 mm down from the heel edge.

Every position is a pair (x, y) in centimetres measured from the back-left corner of top A: x runs along the back wall, y runs along the side wall. Top A covers x 0–249, y 0–65. Top B covers x 0–65, y 65–314. The two tops meet on the line y = 65. Both tops stand 3 cm off their walls.

Your seat is at the L corner, facing it diagonally. The three monitors wrap around that corner. Your knees swing through the open space under the corner and the first 55 cm of each wing; nothing is allowed to stand there.

Heights, from the floor

Optional ALEX feet. The desk is built at 83.0 cm. If you want it higher, four printed round feet under each cabinet (2, 4 or 6 cm; parts/accessories/alex-foot.scad, three #6 × 5/8 in screws each, marked with the ALEX corner template on the bottom corners) raise everything by their height. Nothing on the desk moves; these four numbers do, and the OLOV pre-set in section 3 becomes the table’s number. Decide before section 3.

1 · Parts, tools and the printed helpers

Bought

Fasteners (every screw, with how deep it goes)

No fastener goes deeper than 21 mm into a top. Every depth below respects that. If your tops measure 27 mm instead of 28, shorten each screw into the top by 1 mm.

Tools

The printed helpers

Print everything in parts/tools and the eight rings in parts/structure before you start; parts/README.md has quantities and print settings. Two rules: never scale a printed tool in the slicer (its distances are the tool), and the three marking tools print lip-up and are turned over to use, so the lip hangs over an edge and the label reads correctly.

Setting the drill guide. Loosen the chuck. Drop the 1/8 in bit tip-first into the SET hole marked 1/8 until it bottoms, rest the chuck nose on the top of the tower, and tighten the chuck. The bit now sticks out exactly 45 mm. For the 5/64 in bit use the hole marked 5/64 (35 mm). Do this again every time you change bits. To drill: stand the step you need flat on the surface, put the bit down its bore, and drill until the chuck nose touches the step. The hole is then exactly the depth written on that step. Steps: 12 joining plates, 15 leg and feet plates, 12 ALEX (5/64 bit) rings into the ALEX, 25 RAIL through the angle into the top.

2 · Steel: drill both rails on the bench

Everything in steel is drilled on the bench, before the rails go anywhere near the tops. When you are under the desk later you only drive screws and bolts; you never drill steel there.

Two identical pieces of angle arrive cut to 213 cm; either can be rail A. Tape a label on one end of each: rail A wall end, rail B corner end. All stations below are measured from that labelled end.

Screw holes, in the flat leg

Rail A, drawn opened out flat as if you unfolded the L along the heel; the heavy line between the strips is the heel. Upper strip: the flat leg with its screw holes. Lower strip: the vertical leg with the fitting bolt holes at the measured slot centres, 2.9 and 7.8 cm from each fitting's heel; the fitting with the slot plug is still the template you drill through.

Rail B. Holes 1 and 2 are only 5 cm apart and both land in top A later; holes 3 to 10 land in top B.

Bolt holes for the corner fittings, in the vertical leg

The fitting is the template: clamp it to the rail where it will finally sit, drop a slot plug into each slot and drill the 1/8 in pilot through the plug. The plug matters: the fitting's holes are 21 × 12 mm slots and the bolts 3/8, so a bare punch could land anywhere in 11 mm of slot. The slot centres are 29.15 and 78.45 mm from the heel (measured), so the holes land there. Both fittings are identical, so holes punched through one fit the other. Remember that the heel of a fitting means the outside of its bend.

Where to clamp the fitting as a template, three times. Its heel (outside of the bend) goes on the blue mark, its holes on the 29 mm bolt line, its legs pointing the way shown. The gold dots are where the holes land with the measured slot centres 2.9 and 7.8 cm from the heel. Rail B's two holes serve both fittings.

  1. Scribe the screw line on each flat leg: 19 mm in from the free edge, full length. The printed marking gauge does this: lay it on the flat leg with its lip over the free edge, pencil in the 19 notch, slide. Then scribe the bolt line on each vertical leg the same way: lip over the heel edge, pencil in the 29 notch.
  2. Mark the stations from the labelled end with the tape and square each one across the flat leg with the T-square's edge; you only need the line, the jig finds the 19.
  3. Hook the rail drill jig over the free edge (lip down), slide it until its sight grooves sit on the station line, hold it down and drill the 1/8 in pilot through its bore: square and centred, no punch needed. All 19 stations.
  4. Jig off. Open every pilot to 7/32 in, slow speed, a drop of oil on each; the bit follows the pilot. Deburr both sides.
  5. Rail A, fitting holes:
    1. Clamp a fitting to the vertical leg with its heel exactly on the 52.0 cm mark, legs pointing toward the far end, holes centred on the bolt line. Plug and pilot both holes.
    2. Move it so its heel is on the 51.5 cm mark, legs pointing toward the wall end, holes on the bolt line. Plug and pilot.
    3. Fitting off. Open the four pilots to 7/16 in with the cobalt bit, slow, oil on each (1/4 in in between if the drill labours).
  6. Rail B, fitting holes: clamp the fitting with its heel flush with the corner end of the rail, legs pointing toward the far end, holes on the bolt line. Plug, pilot, open to 7/16 in the same way, two holes.
  7. Floor test. Lay both rails flat legs down, vertical legs up. Butt rail B's corner end against the outer face of rail A's vertical leg so that rail B's outer face is on the 52.0 mark (its flat leg then covers 46.9–52). Bolt both fittings on loosely: the outside one on the far-end side of rail B, the inside one on the wall side. Every bolt should slide in freely. If one is off by a millimetre, open its hole with a round file now, not under the desk.
  8. Take the fittings off. Degrease with acetone, scuff with 120 grit, two light coats of flat black enamel, 24 h to cure. Holes clear themselves; mask nothing.

Marking with the T-square

Turn the T-square over so its lip hangs down, hook the lip over the edge named in the table, slide it to the station, and punch through the named hole. One row for every mark on this top:

  1. Legs L1, L2, L3.
    1. Punch the three centres with the T-square as in the table above (or go straight to the jig in step 3).
    2. Draw a cross through each punch mark with the combination square, each arm about 7 cm long.
    3. Hook the leg jig over the same edge (lip down), slide it to the station, and start the five pilots through its 100 pattern (150 for L1) with the 1/8 in bit, 3 to 4 mm deep. Lift the jig and finish each with the 15 LEG puck. The small centre hole is for a scriber only. The plate's five screws then sit the same way round on every leg.
    4. Drill the four pilots with the drill guide's 15 LEG step and the 1/8 in bit.
    5. Drive the IKEA M4 × 16 mm screws, clutch low, until the heads seat.
    6. Pre-set each leg to 80.2 cm from plate face to foot (or the optional-feet table’s number in section 0b).
  2. Feet F1–F4. F2 and F4: drop the feet corner jig over the far back corner and the far front corner (fences against both edges) and start the four pilots through its plate holes with the 1/8 in bit, 3 to 4 mm deep. F1 and F3: T-square as in the table, scriber through hole 85 to check the station, then the same four pilots through its plate holes. The patterns are the measured 46.8 mm, so every plate lands square with its edges parallel to the top's. Lift the jig and finish the four pilots with the 15 LEG puck. Drive #8 × 5/8 in screws. Pad pointing away from the top. Wind each pad out until the printed foot height gauge (102.0 mm inside) just slides over the foot from the side with no rock: all eight identical, no ruler.
  3. Clamp zones C1 and C2. Nothing goes in x 36–44 along the back edge, 9 cm in from the edge toward the middle, or in y 36–44 along the side-wall end, 9 cm in. Draw the outlines of the two pad plates where the drawing shows them; the plates themselves go in at section 7, no screws.
  4. Rail A.
    1. Draw the rail line at y = 53 from x = 1 to 214.
    2. Clamp the rail with the outer face of its vertical leg on that line, flat leg toward the back edge (it covers y 47.9–53), wall end at x = 1, one centimetre in from the taped end.
    3. Measure 12 cm from the front edge to the vertical leg's outer face at both ends and in the middle. Adjust until all three read 12.0.
    4. Through the rail's two end holes, drill 1/8 in pilots with the drill guide's 25 RAIL step standing on the flat leg (4.8 mm of steel plus 20 mm into the top). Drive #10 × 1 in screws with the Robertson bit on the 3 in extension so the chuck clears the vertical leg; stop when the heads seat. Re-check the 12 cm.
    5. Drill and drive the other seven the same way. Nine screws.
  5. Joining plates wait for section 6. Draw their outlines now (y 53.3–78.7 on this top's scale, so 53.3 to 65 here) so nothing else lands there.

5 · ALEX units and locating rings

The feet pads never touch bare laminate. Each pad drops into a printed ring screwed to the ALEX top, so the top cannot slide on the units and the units hold the whole desk steady.

ALEX 1 top (36 × 58), drawers toward you. Ring centres 5 cm in from each corner.

ALEX 2 top (58 × 36), door toward you. The ring pattern is the same from every corner, so which way the unit faces does not matter for marking.

  1. The ALEX top panel measured 5/8 in (15.9 mm, 23 Oct 2026): the 5/8 in screws reach 13.4 mm into it, leaving 2.5 mm. Fit the rings by tracing the pads with the top standing on the bare cabinet (section 6), not by measuring; the corner template is the check.
  2. Mark each ALEX corner with the printed corner template, turned over so its fences hang down: drop it over the corner with both fences against the edges, start the three pilots through it with the 5/64 in bit, lift it, finish with the 12 ALEX puck. Eight corners; the centre hole is for a scriber only.
  3. Set the 5/64 in bit in the drill guide's 5/64 SET hole and drill the three pilots at each corner with the 12 ALEX step.
  4. Set each ring so its three flange holes sit over the three pilot holes (it only fits one way round) and drive #6 × 5/8 in flat-head screws.
  5. Place the units by the walls, because the tops are not up yet to measure from. ALEX 1: its back 6.5 cm off the back wall (the wall gauge's 65 leg just fits), its near side 212.5 cm from the side wall, drawers facing you. ALEX 2: its side 6.5 cm off the side wall, its near end 277.5 cm from the back wall, door facing you. That is 3.5 cm inside the tops' edges all round once the tops are on. Painter's tape on the floor saves guessing.

6 · Standing it up (two people)

The outside corner of the rails is where rail A's outer face meets rail B's outer face; you can see it from the knee space. The inside corner is the pocket between rail A's outer face and the inner face of rail B's vertical leg, hidden under rail B's flat leg. Both fittings bolt into these two pockets.
  1. Top A. It already has its legs and feet on it. With a helper, turn it over and stand it on its legs, lowering the ALEX end first so the four pads drop into their rings, then the leg end. It cannot be pushed once the pads are in the rings. If the pads miss, or the top is not 3 cm off a wall, lift the ALEX end a few millimetres and slide the ALEX unit, not the top; a 3 cm offcut held against the wall is the easiest gauge. Then level along x and along y, turning the OLOV feet and the round feet until it reads flat and all four pads bear with no rock. Target 83.0 cm at every corner.
  2. Top B. Wind its OLOV legs 2–3 mm short so rail B's corner end can slide under top A. Turn it over onto its legs with its pads in the rings on ALEX 2, push it until its end meets A's front edge and rail B's end touches rail A's vertical leg, 3 cm off the side wall. Wind the legs up until the two surfaces are level at the joint: a fingertip across the joint should find no step. Level by turning the feet, never by packing anything under the joining plates.
  3. Check under the corner before anything is screwed. Rail B's end must touch the face of rail A's vertical leg with no gap, and rail B's outer face must line up with the 52.0 mark on rail A. If not, lift top B a little and move it. Only then go on.
  4. Joining plates. From below, screw P1 and P2 across the joint on the outlines you drew. They are not centred on the joint: each runs y 53.3–78.7, 3 mm clear of rail A, so its centre is 1 cm into top B. Each plate has four holes along its length (2.0, 7.0, 18.4 and 23.4 cm from one end); they land at y 55.3 and 60.3 in top A and 71.7 and 76.7 in top B, all more than 4 cm from an edge, so all four get a screw. Pilot with the drill guide's 12 PLATE step, held up overhead; drive #10 × 5/8 in screws with washers.
  5. Rail B meets rail A. Drill rail B's holes 1 and 2 into top A with the 25 RAIL step and screw.
  6. Corner fittings. The holes already match from section 2.
    1. Outside fitting: heel in the outside corner, one leg flat on rail A's vertical leg toward the far end, the other on rail B's outer face, gusset facing the open knee space.
    2. Inside fitting: heel in the inside corner, one leg on rail A's vertical leg toward the wall, the other on the inner face of rail B's vertical leg, and its gusset pointing at the floor (gusset-up it would sit a millimetre under the head of rail B's first screw; the hole pattern is symmetric, so the holes still line up).
    3. Rail A: four 3/8-16 × 1½ in bolts from the fitting side, washer under each head, washer and nylon-insert nut on the inner face of the leg, under the flat leg.
    4. Rail B: two 3/8-16 × 1½ in bolts through fitting, leg and fitting, washer each end, nylon-insert nut.
    5. Tighten fully, as the glossary's Tight defines it, and only after rail B's end touches rail A and the two surfaces are flush. The mending plates align the tops; they do not carry the corner.

The inside corner in plan view, x to the right and y down as in the plan views (sections 3 and 4 are the mirrored ones). Rail B's end is sandwiched between the two fittings. Gold: the 3/8 in bolts. Black dots: the #10 rail screws, which are in the flat legs, one storey up from the bolts.

7 · Monitor mount and what goes where

The crossbar measured 19¾ in (50.2 cm) centre to centre of its end collars, so that is the pole spacing; VIVO’s 23 in on the box is clamp edge to clamp edge and agrees (19.75 + 3). The pole centre measured 4.37 cm in from the edge, so clamps at 40 cm from the corner put the pole centres 50.2 cm apart to within a millimetre. Fit the crossbar as the spacing guide and tighten the clamps with it on; it sets the last millimetre.
  1. Lay a spare mending plate flat on the underside on each pad outline from section 3, long side along the edge (x 20.0–45.4 at clamp 1, y 20.0–45.4 at clamp 2).
  2. Hang both VIVO lower clamps on their marks, finger-tight only: clamp 1 centred at x = 40 on A's back edge, clamp 2 centred at y = 40 on A's side-wall end. Use the VIVO instruction sheet's Option 1 holes (2 and 4), since the 28 mm top plus plate is under 60 mm. The clamp backs sit in the 3 cm wall gap.
  3. Fit the poles and slide the crossbar onto them as the spacing guide (VIVO sheet step 3). Only now tighten the clamps. Then refit the crossbar at monitor height (VIVO sheet step 6).
  4. Hang the screens and set the heights in the table below. Toe the Acers in about 15°; their near ends end up about 12 cm from the G5's curved ends, and all three screens are landscape.

8 · Final checks

  • Top surface 83.0 cm at all six outer corners, ±3 mm.
  • A level across the joint reads flat both ways; a fingertip finds no step.
  • Corner load test, a known weight rather than a shove: set 20 kg (a case of water plus a few books) on top B's inside corner, the case's two near faces 10 cm from the corner's two edges. Lay a straightedge flat on the surface from above leg L1 toward the inside corner, hold it down over L1, and read the gap under its far end at the corner before and after: no more than 2 mm of drop (the estimate for 20 kg is about 1). Lift the weight off: the corner comes back, nothing creaks, no screw or bolt has moved. Never stand or sit on the corner.
  • Push the desk sideways along each wall: no sway at the top beyond a few millimetres, no movement of a top on its ALEX.
  • No screw tip visible or felt as a bump on either laminate face.
  • Knees clear: sit at the corner, slide in, nothing within reach of a knee or thigh. The rails' lower edge is 75.1 cm from the floor, 12 cm back from the edge.
  • Both ALEX fronts open fully without touching a foot or a leg.
  • All eight feet pads bear in their rings: try to turn each pad by hand; none spins free or rocks.
  • Straightedge across the joint at the inside corner: write down any step now, check it again at two weeks and one month, and retighten the six fitting bolts if the corner has settled.
  • Retighten every screw after two weeks.
If something goes wrong. A screw that spins: back it out, fill the hole with wood glue and a couple of matchsticks, let it dry, re-pilot. A mark off by more than 2 mm: redraw it; a stray 1/8 in hole in the underside does no harm. A fitting bolt that will not go in: open the hole in the rail with a round file, never force it.
