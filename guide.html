0 · How this desk works, in plain words

Read this once before you pick up a drill. Everything after it is a list of numbers; this is why the numbers are what they are.

  1. Two kitchen counter tops make an L. Top A lies along the back wall. Top B lies along the side wall and its end pushes against the front edge of top A. They are the same size; only their direction differs.
  2. What holds them up. At the two far ends of the L, each top rests on an ALEX cabinet. Along the two wall edges there are six IKEA OLOV legs. That leaves the front edges, the ones your knees go under, with nothing beneath them for over two metres. Counter tops sag and eventually break like that.
  3. So each front edge gets a steel rail. A piece of steel angle, 2 inches on each side and 213 cm long, is screwed to the underside of each top, 12 cm back from the front edge. Steel on edge is very stiff; the rails carry the front edges the way a beam carries a floor. Where the two rails meet at the inside corner they are bolted to each other with two heavy steel corner fittings, so the corner of top B hangs from rail A instead of from nothing.
  4. Why the cabinets have feet on them. An ALEX is 70 cm tall. You want the desk surface at 83 cm. Each cabinet therefore carries four 10 cm adjustable feet between its top and the desk. The feet are screwed to the desk, and their round pads drop into little printed rings screwed to the cabinet. The rings stop the desk sliding on the cabinets, and because the cabinets are heavy boxes on the floor, that also stops the whole desk from rocking sideways; the thin OLOV legs could not do that on their own.
  5. Why the legs are where they are. All six legs stand on the wall edges, none under the front. That keeps the space under the corner, where you sit, completely open for your knees. One leg sits right under the monitor mount's second clamp, because that clamp carries the weight of the screens.
  6. Why no screw goes deeper than 21 mm. The tops are 28 mm particleboard with a thin laminate skin. A screw that reaches the skin leaves a bump you will see and feel for ever. Every screw length in this guide was chosen so that it stops at least 7 mm short of the top face. Keep that rule if you ever substitute a screw.
  7. Why the tops are drawn mirrored. You mark the undersides with the tops face down. Looking at a face-down top you see its layout flipped left-to-right, like a mirror. Sections 3 and 4 draw each top exactly as you will see it on the blanket, so you can hold the page next to the top and the drawing matches.

Words used in this guide

Angle
Steel bent into an L. One side is 2 inches, the other side is 2 inches, the steel is 3/16 inch thick.
Flat leg
The side of the angle that lies flat against the underside of the top. All the screws go through it.
Vertical leg
The side of the angle that hangs straight down toward the floor. The corner fittings bolt to it.
Heel
The bend, where the two legs meet. The heel edge is the top edge of the vertical leg, flush with the underside of the desk.
Free edge
The outer edge of a leg, the one that is not the heel. The screw holes sit 19 mm in from the flat leg's free edge.
Station
A position along a rail, measured from one labelled end. Rail A is measured from its wall end; rail B from its corner end.
Pilot hole
A small hole drilled first so the screw goes in straight and does not split the board. In these tops it is always a 1/8 in bit.
Tape flag
A piece of masking tape wrapped round the drill bit at a set distance from the tip. When the tape touches the surface, stop. It is your depth gauge.
Centre punch
A pointed steel tool (a nail works). Tap it on a mark on steel to make a dent, so the drill bit starts exactly there instead of skating.
Deburr
Drilling leaves a sharp ring of metal around a hole. Rub it off with a file or twist a bigger bit in the hole by hand.
Snug
Tight enough that nothing moves, then stop. Not "as hard as you can".
Toe-in
Turning a side monitor or speaker a little toward you instead of flat along the wall.
Gusseted fitting
A steel L-shaped bracket with a triangular web across its inside corner. Very stiff. Ours are made for 1-5/8 in strut channel and have two 9/16 in holes in each leg.

The angle rail seen end-on, as it sits under the top, and the two lines everything is measured from: the screw line 19 mm in from the flat leg's free edge, and the bolt line 29 mm down from the heel edge.

2 · Steel: drill both rails on the bench

Everything in steel is drilled on the bench, before the rails go anywhere near the tops. When you are under the desk later you only drive screws and bolts; you never drill steel there.

Two pieces of angle arrive cut to 213 cm. Decide which is rail A and which is rail B, and tape a label on one end of each: rail A wall end, rail B corner end. All stations below are measured from that labelled end.

Screw holes, in the flat leg

Rail A. The upper strip is the flat leg (screw holes), the lower strip is the vertical leg (the fitting bolt holes, drawn where they fall if your fitting's holes are near 3 and 7 cm from its heel; yours set the exact spots).

Rail B. Holes 1 and 2 are only 5 cm apart and both land in top A later; holes 3 to 10 land in top B.

Bolt holes for the corner fittings, in the vertical leg

You do not need to know the fitting's hole spacing. The fitting is the template: clamp it to the rail where it will finally sit, punch through its holes, drill. Both fittings are identical, so holes punched through one fit the other.

Where to clamp the fitting as a template, three times. Its heel goes on the blue mark, its holes 29 mm down from the heel edge of the rail, its legs pointing the way shown. The gold dots are where the holes land if your fitting's holes are about 3 and 7 cm from its heel. Rail B's two holes serve both fittings.

  1. Scribe the screw line on each flat leg: 19 mm in from the free edge, full length, with a combination square. Mark the stations from the labelled end, centre-punch each mark.
  2. Drill every screw hole 7/32 in, slow speed, a drop of oil on each. Deburr both sides.
  3. Rail A, fitting holes: clamp a fitting to the vertical leg with its heel exactly on the 52.0 cm mark, legs pointing toward the far end, holes 29 mm down from the heel edge. Punch through both holes. Move it so its heel is on the 51.5 cm mark, legs pointing toward the wall end, same height. Punch. Drill the four marks 7/16 in.
  4. Rail B, fitting holes: clamp the fitting with its heel flush with the corner end of the rail, legs pointing toward the far end, holes 29 mm down. Punch, drill 7/16 in, two holes.
  5. Test: lay the two rails on the floor in an L, rail B's end against rail A's vertical leg at the 52 cm mark, and bolt both fittings on loosely. Everything should line up with the bolts sliding in freely. If a hole is off by a millimetre, open it with a round file now, not under the desk.
  6. Take the fittings off. Degrease with acetone, scuff with 120 grit, two light coats of flat black enamel, 24 h to cure. Holes clear themselves; mask nothing.

3 · Top A, face down

Lay top A face down on a blanket. Find the end that will go against the side wall (that is x = 0) and the long edge that will go against the back wall (y = 0). Put a strip of tape on each and label them. Every mark below is measured from those two edges. The drawing is what you see looking down at the face-down top: x grows to the left.

Top A underside as seen face down. Gold circles: OLOV plates (11 cm). Grey squares: feet plates (6 cm). Grey strip: rail A, its screws as dots. Hatched boxes C1, C2: VIVO clamp zones, keep clear. Pale bars: the two loose mending plates that pad the clamp jaws. Blue bars P1, P2: the joining plates, half on this top.

  1. Legs L1, L2, L3. Mark each centre. Set the OLOV plate on the mark, scribe through its four holes, pilot 1/8 in, 15 mm deep (tape flag at 15 mm). Drive the IKEA M4 × 16 mm screws. Pre-set each leg to 80.2 cm from plate face to foot.
  2. Feet F1–F4. Mark each centre; the 6 cm plate sits centred on it with its edges parallel to the top's edges. Scribe the four plate holes, pilot 1/8 in, 15 mm deep, drive #8 × 5/8 in screws. Pad pointing away from the top. Wind each pad out until the foot measures 10.2 cm overall.
  3. Clamp zones C1 and C2. Nothing goes in x 31–39 along the back edge, 9 cm in, or in y 31–39 along the left end, 9 cm in. Lay the two spare mending plates loose where drawn; they go between the clamp jaw and the top in section 7, no screws.
  4. Rail A. Draw the rail line at y = 53 from x = 1 to 214. Clamp the rail with the outer face of its vertical leg on that line, flat leg toward the back edge (it covers y 48–53), wall end at x = 1. Through each of the nine holes drill a 1/8 in pilot 20 mm into the top: tape flag 25 mm from the bit tip (4.8 mm of steel plus 20 mm). Drive #10 × 1 in screws with the Robertson bit on the 3 in extension so the chuck clears the vertical leg; stop when the head seats.
  5. Joining plates wait for section 6. Mark their outlines now so nothing else lands there.

5 · ALEX units and locating rings

The feet pads never touch bare laminate. Each pad drops into a printed ring screwed to the ALEX top, so the top cannot slide on the units and the units hold the whole desk steady.

ALEX 1 top (36 × 58), drawers toward you. Ring centres 5 cm in from each corner.

ALEX 2 top (58 × 36), door toward you. Same rule.

  1. Print eight rings from parts/foot-ring.stl: 51 mm bore, 3 mm wall, 10 mm tall ring on a 75 mm flange, three countersunk holes in the flange for #6 × 5/8 in flat-head screws, open bottom so the pad sits on the ALEX laminate. PETG or ABS, flange down, 3 perimeters. Measure your pad first with calipers; if it is not 50.0 mm, change pad_d in parts/foot-ring.scad and re-render.
  2. Mark the four ring centres on each ALEX top, 5 cm in from each corner. Set each ring on its mark, scribe the three holes, pilot 5/64 in 12 mm deep, drive the screws.
  3. Place the units: ALEX 1 at x 209.5–245.5, y 3.5–61.5 with the drawers facing you; ALEX 2 at x 3.5–61.5, y 274.5–310.5 with the door facing you. Both 3 cm off their walls. Painter's tape on the floor at x = 209.5 and y = 274.5 saves guessing.

6 · Standing it up (two people)

  1. Top A. Lift it onto L1–L3 with the four pads dropping into the rings on ALEX 1. Lift and set; the rings do not allow a push. Final position: 3 cm off both walls. Level along x and along y. Adjust the OLOV feet and the round feet until it reads flat and all four pads bear with no rock. Target 83.0 cm at every corner.
  2. Top B. Lift onto L4–L6 with its pads in the rings on ALEX 2, its end against A's front edge, 3 cm off the side wall. Adjust until the two surfaces are flush at the joint: run a fingertip across; shim the legs, never the plates.
  3. Joining plates. From below, screw P1 and P2 across the joint: each plate centred on the joint line, long axis across it, two #10 × 5/8 in screws with washers in each top, pilots 1/8 in, 12 mm deep. Use the plate's holes nearest 4 cm and 9 cm from the joint on each side.
  4. Rail B meets rail A. Push rail B's corner end against rail A's vertical leg. Drill holes 1 and 2 into top A (25 mm flag) and screw.
  5. Corner fittings. The holes already match from section 2. Outside fitting: heel in the outside corner, one leg on rail A's vertical leg toward the far end, the other on rail B's outer face, gusset facing the open space. Inside fitting: heel in the inside corner, one leg on rail A's vertical leg toward the wall, the other on the inner face of rail B's vertical leg, gusset tucked under B's flat leg. Rail A: four 3/8-16 × 1 in bolts from the fitting side, washer under each head, washer and nylon-insert nut inside the leg. Rail B: two 3/8-16 × 1½ in bolts through fitting, leg and fitting. Snug, not cranked.

The inside corner in plan view (x right, y down, as in every other drawing). Rail B's end is sandwiched between the two fittings. Gold: the 3/8 in bolts. Black dots: the #10 rail screws, which are in the flat legs, one storey up from the bolts.

7 · Monitor mount and what goes where

The two VIVO clamp positions depend on the crossbar's real pole spacing (section 9). The numbers below assume 42 cm; if the crossbar measures 54–58 cm collar to collar, both clamps move out to about 43–46 cm from the corner and nothing else changes.
  1. Lay a spare mending plate flat on the underside at each clamp zone. Fit the VIVO lower clamps using the manual's Option 1 holes (2 and 4), since the 28 mm top plus plate is under 60 mm.
  2. Clamp 1 centred at x = 35 on A's back edge; clamp 2 centred at y = 35 on A's left end edge. The clamp backs sit in the 3 cm wall gap.
  3. Slide the crossbar onto the poles as the spacing guide (VIVO step 3), tighten the clamps, then refit the crossbar at monitor height (VIVO step 6).
  4. Hang the screens and set the heights in the table below. Toe the Acers in about 15° so their near ends sit about 6 cm from the M7's ends.

8 · Final checks

  • Top surface 83.0 cm at all six outer corners, ±3 mm.
  • A level across the joint reads flat both ways; a fingertip finds no step.
  • Push the inside corner down hard: it gives 2–3 mm and springs back, no creak.
  • Push the desk sideways along each wall: no sway at the top beyond a few millimetres, no movement of a top on its ALEX.
  • No screw tip visible or felt as a bump on either laminate face.
  • Knees clear: sit at the corner, slide in, nothing within reach of a knee or thigh. The rails' lower edge is 75.1 cm from the floor, 12 cm back from the edge.
  • Both ALEX fronts open fully without touching a foot or a leg.
  • Retighten every screw after two weeks.
